The Common Entrance Examination (CEE) is conducted by Nepal's Medical Education Commission (MEC) for admission into health science programs like MBBS, BDS, and B.Sc. Nursing across Nepal's medical colleges. It is held once a year, typically between July and September, and is one of the most competitive entrance exams in Nepal with thousands of students competing for limited seats.

The exam consists of 200 MCQ questions to be completed in 3 hours, covering Physics (50), Chemistry (50), Biology (Zoology + Botany, 80), and a Mental Agility Test (20). Each correct answer carries 1 mark, while an incorrect answer deducts 0.25 marks, making the maximum possible score 200. This negative marking system means that guessing without knowledge can hurt your score, so strategic preparation is essential.

The Biology section, split equally between Zoology and Botany with 40 questions each, carries the highest combined weightage at 80 marks. Human Biology and Physiology alone accounts for 15 questions in Zoology, making it the single highest weightage subchapter across all subjects. On the Chemistry side, Physical Chemistry and Organic Chemistry carry 17 questions each, while in Physics, Modern Physics (12 questions) and Mechanics (10 questions) are the heaviest topics. Knowing where the marks concentrate helps you prioritise your study plan effectively.

Seats in MBBS, BDS, B.Sc. Nursing, BPH, BPT, and other allied health programs are limited and competition is high every year, so a strong, consistent practice routine is essential. This platform is built to give you that routine: free, unlimited practice across every chapter of the CEE syllabus, with solutions that explain the concept behind each answer rather than just the final option. Whether you are starting from scratch or polishing weak topics close to the exam, you can practice at your own pace.

Many students lose marks not because they lack knowledge, but because they run out of time or make careless errors under pressure. Practising with timed chapter wise tests trains you to manage the roughly 50 seconds per question that the real CEE allows. After each test, reviewing the detailed solutions helps you spot conceptual gaps and avoid repeating mistakes. Over time, this cycle of practice, review, and revision builds the speed and accuracy that separate top scorers from the rest.

How to Prepare for CEE Effectively

The most common mistake CEE aspirants make is trying to study everything at once without a plan. A better approach is to break the syllabus into manageable chunks and focus on one or two chapters per week. Start with the highest weightage topics: Human Biology and Physiology (15 questions), Physical Chemistry (17 questions), Organic Chemistry (17 questions), and Modern Physics (12 questions), because mastering these gives you the biggest score improvement for the least effort.

After studying a chapter's theory, immediately test yourself with chapter wise MCQs. This active recall approach strengthens your memory far more than re-reading notes. Keep a dedicated notebook for questions you got wrong, write down the concept you missed, and revisit those questions every week. Students who follow this simple cycle of study, test, review, and revise typically see their scores improve by 15 to 25 marks within a month of consistent practice.

In the final month before the exam, shift from chapter wise study to full length mock tests. Take at least one full test every week under real exam conditions: sit at a desk, set a timer, and do not use your phone. After the test, compare your score with previous attempts using the score history feature to track progress and identify persistent weak areas. The negative marking in CEE means that strategic question selection is just as important as knowledge. If you cannot eliminate at least one option, it is often better to skip the question than to guess and lose 0.25 marks.

Consistency matters more than intensity. Studying for 30 to 45 minutes every day produces better results than cramming for 6 hours once a week. The human brain retains information better through spaced repetition, so revisiting the same topics at regular intervals locks them into long term memory. Use this platform daily, even if only for a quick 20 question chapter test, and you will build the speed, accuracy, and confidence needed to score well on exam day.
