The Night Before CEE: What to Do and What to Avoid

The night before CEE can either set you up for success or undo months of preparation. Here is the time-by-time breakdown I learned the hard way after making every mistake possible on my first attempt.

By Ramu Pandey · Published: August 11, 2026 · 9 min read

Before my first CEE attempt, I believed that the night before was my last chance to learn something new. I opened my textbook at 8 PM and tried to revise Plant Physiology, a topic I had always felt shaky on. I read for two hours, understood maybe half of it, felt anxious about the half I did not understand, and went to bed at midnight with my head spinning. The alarm went off at 5 AM. I had slept five hours, eaten nothing substantial, and walked into the exam hall already behind.

My second attempt was completely different. The night before was calm, deliberate, and short. I was in bed by 10 PM, I had packed everything the evening before, and I woke up feeling rested instead of wrecked. My score improved by 19 marks between the two attempts, and a significant portion of that improvement came from simply being physically and mentally ready on exam day instead of exhausted and anxious.

The mistakes I made on my first night

There are specific things I did wrong before my first CEE that I want to share because they are incredibly common among aspirants. If you recognise any of these patterns, stop them now before they cost you marks.

  • Studying new topics: I tried to revise an entire chapter I had not studied well. This created anxiety, not knowledge. The night before is for consolidation, not acquisition.
  • Staying up late: I slept at midnight and woke at 5 AM. Five hours of sleep before a three-hour exam is a recipe for poor concentration and slow recall.
  • Anxiety spirals: I kept thinking about all the topics I had not covered. This mental loop produced no useful revision and only increased my stress level.
  • Discussing with friends: I called a friend who told me about a Physics topic he had just revised. I had not studied it. This sent me into a panic and I spent an hour trying to learn it at the worst possible time.

Every one of these mistakes was avoidable. Here is the evening routine I followed before my second attempt and the one I recommend to every CEE aspirant I meet.

The packing checklist: Admit card, photo ID, two pens, two pencils, an eraser, a sharpener, a transparent water bottle, a light snack for the break, and your exam centre address written on paper. Put everything in one bag the night before. Check it twice. This eliminates the morning panic of searching for your admit card at 6 AM.

What to eat and what to avoid

Dinner the night before CEE should be simple, familiar, and eaten early. Roti with dal and seasonal vegetables is ideal. Rice with fish or chicken is fine if that is your regular food. The key rule is: do not eat anything you have not eaten before. The night before an exam is not the time to try a new restaurant or a new dish. Your stomach needs to be comfortable, not experimenting.

Avoid excessive caffeine. If you normally drink tea, have your usual cup in the evening but do not add an extra cup to stay awake. Caffeine after 4 PM disrupts sleep quality even if you fall asleep on time. Avoid heavy fried foods, excessive spicy food, and large quantities of sweets. These can cause discomfort that interferes with sleep quality.

For the morning, eat something light and energising. A banana and a handful of biscuits, or two parathas with curd, or toast with peanut butter. Eat at least 90 minutes before the exam starts so your body has time to digest. Do not skip breakfast. Your brain needs glucose for three hours of focused thinking, and an empty stomach guarantees that your concentration will fade by the second hour.

How to actually fall asleep

The hardest part of the night before CEE is falling asleep when your mind is running through topics and scenarios. I have been there. Here are the specific techniques that worked for me.

  • Box breathing: Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4 counts, exhale for 4 counts, hold for 4 counts. Repeat 10 times. This activates your parasympathetic nervous system and reduces heart rate.
  • Body scan: Lie still and focus on each body part from toes to head, consciously relaxing each one. By the time you reach your head, your body is relaxed enough for sleep.
  • No screens for 30 minutes before bed: Phone screens emit blue light that suppresses melatonin. Put your phone in another room if you cannot resist checking it.
  • If you cannot sleep, do not panic: Lying quietly in a dark room is restful even if you are not fully asleep. The rest itself is valuable. Do not start counting hours and panicking about being tired.

The morning routine

Wake up at least two and a half hours before the exam start time. This gives you enough time to shower, eat breakfast, get dressed, and travel to the centre without rushing. Rushing in the morning raises cortisol and puts you in a stressed state before you even see the first question.

Here is my recommended morning timeline for a 9 AM exam start.

The mistake of last-minute discussion

At the exam centre, you will see other students revising notes, discussing topics, and quizzing each other. Resist the urge to join them. If someone mentions a topic you are weak in, it will shake your confidence right before the exam. If someone tells you a formula that you had forgotten, you will spend the first few minutes of the exam trying to remember it instead of focusing on the first questions.

On my second attempt, I arrived at the centre, sat in a quiet corner, and listened to music for ten minutes. I did not open a book. I did not discuss anything with anyone. When the exam started, I was calm, focused, and ready. That mental state is worth more than any last-minute revision.

What I wish I had known

The night before CEE is not about studying more. It is about preparing your body and mind to perform at their best for three consecutive hours. Sleep is preparation. Food is preparation. Calm is preparation. The student who sleeps well, eats well, and arrives relaxed will almost always outperform the student who studied until midnight and arrived exhausted. Your preparation happened over months. The night before is for trusting that preparation and showing up ready to execute it.
